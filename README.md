# Swarms, Farms and Armies

In October 2026, Lula's re-election in Brazil, credited in part to the WhatsApp channel "Exército do Lula" run by congressman André Janones, followed within days by French activist channels built on the same model, revived a question: is this all just Gamergate happening again? This report answers it by tracing online influence campaigns from 2014 to 2026, with reference points from 2010, and sorting them into three lineages that converge in 2026.

<img src="monde.jpg" alt="image-monde" width="100%">

![The three lineages](diagrams/lineages-light.svg#gh-light-mode-only)
![The three lineages](diagrams/lineages-dark.svg#gh-dark-mode-only)

1. **The human swarm**: real participants with no command center, held together by a shared culture (4chan, Anonymous, Gamergate, the "Great Meme War," the Groypers).
2. **The farm**: accounts, websites or people paid or commissioned to simulate an opinion for a state or a client (Internet Research Agency, Doppelgänger, Storm-1516, Team Jorge, paid influencers in Romania).
3. **The directed activist army**: an identifiable center that hands out "missions" to real activists (the BJP IT cell, the "gabinete do ódio," the Insoumis Discord, Janones's channel).

## Method

**Sorting criterion.** Cases are classified with the notion of "coordinated inauthentic behavior" (CIB), formalized by Facebook in 2018. Coordinating is allowed; lying about who you are is not. A setup becomes inauthentic when it lies about the identity of the sender, the number of participants or the sponsor.

**Labels.** Every important fact carries one of three labels. **ATTESTED**: established by a primary source (ruling, indictment, official or platform report, published internal document, the actor's own statement) or by a solid, cross-checked investigation. An indictment is still an accusation. **ALLEGED**: asserted by an interested party, a single source, or a contested attribution. **UNKNOWN**: no public data.

**Effects.** A report that attributes an operation does not measure its effect on the vote. Unless stated otherwise, the electoral effect of the cases described is unknown. Subscriber and "block" counts published by the actors themselves are self-reported.

**Sources.** Public sources up to October 31, 2026: court records, public agencies (Viginum, BSI, OSCE/ODIHR, Brazil's electoral court), platform and AI lab reports (Meta, TikTok, Google, OpenAI, Anthropic, Microsoft), academic research and the press. Every dated fact links to its source. For several events from July to October 2026 only excerpts of paywalled articles were available; the text flags them.

**Deliberate limit.** Tactics are described at the level at which the press, research and public reports describe them. The report contains no procedure for building a network of accounts, automating a campaign or evading detection.

Sourced corrections are welcome through an issue or a pull request.
